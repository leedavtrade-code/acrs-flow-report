RSRelative Strength — how a stock/sector/country is doing vs a benchmark, not its raw price change. The core ranking number everywhere on this page.
13-week RSRS measured over one calendar quarter — long enough to filter noise, short enough to catch a leader while it's still fresh (academic momentum research finds 3–12 months is the sweet spot; under ~1 month tends to reverse, over ~12 tends to be stale news).

It deliberately ignores the 1M and 3M columns, so a market can be down sharply this quarter and still be S2 Advancing if it is well above a rising 150-day average. Likewise a +6% bounce inside a falling 150-day MA is still S4 Declining.

Each sector plotted by strength (RS-Ratio, → right = stronger) and momentum (RS-Momentum, ↑ up = gaining). Four quadrants: Leading (strong & rising) · Weakening (strong, fading) · Lagging (weak) · Improving (weak, turning up). Sectors rotate clockwise. Colour matches direction: green ↗ = improving on both axes, red ↙ = fading on both, amber = mixed (e.g. a leader rolling over points amber down-right). Why the ★ doesn't move when you change timeframe: the table re-sorts by whichever timeframe you pick, but the ★ (which sectors actually feed the weekly shortlist) is permanently locked to the 13-week / 3M ranking — that's the spec §5 entry gate, and it never changes with the view. So on the 1M or 1Y view you'll often see the #1-sorted sector without a ★, while a lower-sorted sector keeps it. That's correct, not a bug. The "3M rank" column shows the number the ★ is based on.

Why it's fixed at 13 weeks: one quarter is long enough to filter noise but short enough to catch leadership while it's fresh — and locking it stops the system from chasing whatever timeframe happens to look good this week.

Weekly shortlist ?

v1.5 model: only relative-strength leaders (13-wk RS ≥ 15% vs SPY). How you exit — this is the whole job: check each evening; on any daily close below the 50-day MA, sell at the next morning's open. Nothing else closes a position in normal conditions. The stop-market at entry −2.5×ATR% is gap insurance only — set once, never moved. Two alternatives are deliberately not used, both measured: a trailing stop fires on the first pullback and replaces the MA exit entirely (10y CAGR 2.3% vs 18.0% — it exits 88% of positions on the stop instead of 27%); a profit target caps the upside the system runs on (selling half at +1R/+2R/+3R lost 0 of 5 periods, P=0.031, while winners keep running +8.1% for 60 days after exit). Portfolio caps (obey these — they cap true risk): max 6 open positions · max 2 per sector · 1% risk each. Because the shortlist is concentrated in the top-5 sectors, positions are correlated — treat 6 names in 3 sectors as ~one 4–6% bet, not 6% of independent risk. Expect losing streaks of 8–10 in a row (a ~39%-win trend system); that's normal, don't abandon it mid-streak. Regime gate: "Setup B blocked" appears when SPY closes below its 150-day MA — pullback entries are suppressed in downtrends (backtested). It clears automatically when SPY reclaims the 150-day MA. See the spec for full rules. It is a research input, not a system: there is no entry rule, no sizing rule, and no exit rule behind these names. Treat them as candidates to investigate, not signals.

Sector — price vs modelled value ?

Every valued S&P 500 member is aggregated by sector: market cap = Σ(price × shares), modelled cap = Σ(intrinsic value × shares). The ratio is how far the sector's price sits above (>1.0) or below (<1.0) what the models can justify. Using all valued members matters — aggregating only the Buy list would make every sector look cheap by construction.
The models have known blind spots. Graham and DCF structurally undervalue asset-light, high-growth businesses and misprice banks, insurers and REITs, so a high ratio in Technology or a low one in Financials is partly the model, not the market.
