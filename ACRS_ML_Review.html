Critic: what the ledger says

  1. Setup B carries the system: 71 of 355 trades (20%) produce +68.0R of +140.5R. Without its 3 best trades it still makes -14.6R and it was positive in 4 of 6 years (bootstrap P(not better than the rest) = 0.349).
  2. Setup C is 222 trades (62% of all entries) for +5.3R in total (-15.2R without its best 3). It occupies slots and risk budget for roughly zero edge; this is a hypothesis to test in the builder at portfolio level, not a rule change.
  3. Disaster-stop and gap exits cost -43.8R. Those entries were stretched: 15.7% above MA50 vs 3.8% for normal exits, RVOL 1.82 vs 1.38.
  4. Returns are tail-driven: the top 5% of trades supply 75% of gross profit. Any filter that skips entries has to be judged on R kept, not on hit rate.
  5. Live window: 21 closed trades, -6.65R, PF 0.24. Drawing 21 backtest trades that could have closed inside a 76-day window gives a result this bad 13% of the time (PF this low: 12%): normal for a young book. Comparing against all backtest trades, long winners included, would say 5% and wrongly suggest something broke. Only 4 Setup A entries vs 3.6 expected (P = 0.71); the live book has mostly been fed the setups that earn ~0R in the backtest too.
  6. Entry-feature drift vs backtest: none significant after Bonferroni (p < 0.0045). The machine is buying the same kind of stocks.
  7. ML entry filter (keep top 50% by model score), purged annual walk-forward: take-all +146.4R vs ridge +71.1R (IC -0.075) and logistic +59.3R (IC -0.017). Status: rejected - stays record-only.
  8. Deflated Sharpe: the 5-year curve's Sharpe 1.13 gives DSR 0.74 at the registry's 19 trials; it drops below 0.95 at about 4 independent trials. This is before the survivorship look-ahead the simulation page already measures, which lowers it further.

Step 3 · Deflated Sharpe: how many tries can this backtest afford?

Sharpe 1.13 on 1254 daily returns (skew 0.48, kurtosis 11.57); PSR vs zero = 0.9946. The hurdle is the Sharpe that the best of N zero-edge variants would show by luck. Every variant you or an AI agent tries raises N. That is why an agent that rewrites the rules and re-backtests in a loop will always find a 'better' strategy: it is spending this budget. Survivorship look-ahead is not in this number; the simulation page measures that separately. With the hurdle set from the parameter grid's own Sharpe spread instead, DSR = 0.986. That figure is optimistic, because neighbouring parameters are near-copies of each other.
