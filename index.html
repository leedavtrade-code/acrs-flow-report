RSRelative Strength — how a stock/sector/country is doing vs a benchmark, not its raw price change. The core ranking number everywhere on this page.
13-week RSRS measured over one calendar quarter — long enough to filter noise, short enough to catch a leader while it's still fresh (academic momentum research finds 3–12 months is the sweet spot; under ~1 month tends to reverse, over ~12 tends to be stale news).
RVOLRelative Volume — today's volume ÷ its own 20-day average. Confirms a breakout isn't happening on a quiet, meaningless day.
ATRAverage True Range — the average size of a stock's daily price swing. Sets the disaster-stop distance (2.5×ATR%) so it fits that stock's own normal noise.

Sector ranking ?

Two different questions, so two tabs.
Global — ranks world sectors by how the median member stock performed over 13 weeks. It decides which sectors' stocks may enter the weekly shortlist. It buys nothing itself.
US sleeve — ranks the US sector ETFs by their own 12-month price return, and holds the top 3. This one is a real position.
Different measure, different window, different job — so the two tabs often disagree, and a sector can rank last on one while being held on the other. That is expected, not an error.

Why the ★ doesn't move when you change timeframe: the table re-sorts by whichever timeframe you pick, but the ★ (which sectors actually feed the weekly shortlist) is permanently locked to the 13-week / 3M ranking — that's the spec §5 entry gate, and it never changes with the view. So on the 1M or 1Y view you'll often see the #1-sorted sector without a ★, while a lower-sorted sector keeps it. That's correct, not a bug. The "3M rank" column shows the number the ★ is based on.

Why it's fixed at 13 weeks: one quarter is long enough to filter noise but short enough to catch leadership while it's fresh — and locking it stops the system from chasing whatever timeframe happens to look good this week.
What this sleeve holds. ?
This is a risk-control sleeve, not a return upgrade. Measured 2015-10 to 2026-08 on the same window as everything else: rotation 12.80% CAGR / Sharpe 0.93 / max drawdown -15.90% versus SPY 15.43% / 0.87 / -33.72%. It loses to the index on return and roughly halves the drawdown. Return per unit of drawdown is 0.80 against SPY's 0.46. If you want the highest return, hold SPY. This sleeve is for holding equities through a drawdown you can actually sit through.

The rule: at each month end, rank 9 sector ETFs by their 252-day return, hold the top 3 equal-weight, but only sectors whose own 252-day return is positive. Any unfilled slot goes to IEF (7-10y Treasuries). That absolute-momentum gate is what supplies the crash protection - it made +3.4% in 2022 while SPY lost 18.6%.

Timing: the allocation shown was decided at the last completed month end and applies to the month after it. It is never computed from a part-finished month - doing that is what inflates backtests, and it was measured here to be worth +9.7pp of fake CAGR.

Sector — price vs modelled value ?

Every valued S&P 500 member is aggregated by sector: market cap = Σ(price × shares), modelled cap = Σ(intrinsic value × shares). The ratio is how far the sector's price sits above (>1.0) or below (<1.0) what the models can justify. Using all valued members matters — aggregating only the Buy list would make every sector look cheap by construction.
The models have known blind spots. Graham and DCF structurally undervalue asset-light, high-growth businesses and misprice banks, insurers and REITs, so a high ratio in Technology or a low one in Financials is partly the model, not the market.
